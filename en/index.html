The Black Box

Musta laatikko – Tekoälyttömyyden ajan alku
(The Beginning of the Age of Non-Intelligence)

The Black Box is a journey of exploration into a world where algorithms, artificial intelligence and invisible systems shape our emotions, our choices and our communities.

We have entered the Age of Non-Intelligence: either we become the foolish users of smart machines, or we learn to use them wisely. AI works inside a black box, a system whose workings we cannot see but whose effects we feel. What happens when AI becomes a teacher, a friend and a mirror?

If AI chose wisely, would you let it decide about your life? Or your death? This book invites you to look beneath the surface.

Who is Jarno Alastalo?

When Suomi24 had more than a million users, Jarno Alastalo saw up close how people behave when there is only a username behind the screen.

He is a techno-philosopher, author, builder of digital communities and expert on cat videos who has designed black boxes. He examines technology from a human perspective: how artificial intelligence, social media and algorithms shape humanity, identity and values.

Behind this is a long career as a journalist. Jarno co-founded the Suomi24 community, led Aller Media’s online business and built the Heimo community service, where even introverts dared to open up. Today his company MPH Making People Happy develops concepts and content for digital services and social media platforms, and his thinking workshop CSY Lab explores the interface between algorithms, artificial intelligence and humanity.

His latest book is Musta laatikko – Tekoälyttömyyden ajan alku (The Black Box – The Beginning of the Age of Non-Intelligence, 2026). Before it came the true-story books Oudot tyypit (Strange People, 2020), Pimeä netti (The Dark Net, 2017) and Sometarinoita Suomesta (Stories from Social Media, 2015). All are in Finnish.

His lectures have been heard by, among others, media executives, government ministries, staff of large corporations, youth workers, teachers, students, social and healthcare professionals and local residents.

His talks do not explain code. They ask what happens to us when the machine starts thinking for us.

As a Speaker

The oldest written story in the world begins with a king who cuts down a forest for his own name. He is warned, and he decides alone. Now forests are cut down to make way for data centres. In Jarno’s talks, AI is neither magic nor doomsday. It is decisions that someone makes, and the question is who makes them. What for? is too important a question to leave to kings.

Jarno’s talks are visual and game-like, and they are built from stories. They contain research and real people, and sometimes the Jarno speaking on the screen is not Jarno. He has spoken to staff of Finland’s largest companies, to government ministries, to upper secondary students and in Mikkeli Prison.

  • Foolish Acts or Artificial Intelligence?

    AI is not magic – but it makes us believe it is. This keynote reveals what AI really is, what it is not – and why it still succeeds in shaping our feelings, thoughts, and decisions.

  • Algorithms as Black Boxes

    What happens when decisions are made by systems whose logic we cannot see or understand? Based on The Black Box (2026), this talk takes audiences deep into the invisible mechanisms of algorithms: how they shape everyday life, culture, and society – and who benefits from them.

  • AI and Identity

    When the machine acts as a mirror, it does not reflect truth but selected, distorted versions of ourselves. What does this mean for identity formation, public debate, and our shared sense of reality?

  • Youth and AI

    Young people are the first generation for whom AI has been part of daily life from birth. Algorithms recommend content, suggest words, and decide who is visible to whom. Through practical examples and young people’s own experiences, this talk dismantles myths and explores what kind of literacy is needed to understand AI – and what remains essentially human.

  • Digital Oligarchs and the Concentration of Power

    A handful of tech companies now hold the power to define what we see and feel. How does this change democracy, values, and the future? And what alternatives do we have to build a different digital culture?

  • AI, Ethics and Humanity

    Machines make mistakes, but they do not understand them. They feed us cognitive sugar and learn our weaknesses before we even recognize them ourselves. What ethical questions does this raise in education, decision-making, and everyday life?

  • Digital Identities, Loneliness and Communities

    How do identities created online meet real life? Why doesn’t social media remove loneliness – and how might digital communities truly support us?

  • What Does a Prompt Cost?

    How much water does AI use? Nobody knows exactly, and the numbers make young people feel guilty, as if the problem were their own use. The big decisions are made elsewhere. The talk gives those who work with young people three tools: ask why the young person asks. Zoom out: what is this for, who pays and who decides? And ask for another way, from the people who decide. When the ozone layer was in danger, the world did not give up fridges. It changed the gas, together.

Working with Young People

My specialty is speaking to educators, youth workers, and teachers about how AI and algorithms shape young people’s everyday lives, identities, and agency. My talks combine research, young people’s perspectives, and practical examples that provide tools for understanding machine logic and supporting critical thinking in the AI era.

For nearly a decade I have collaborated with the Centre of Expertise for Digital Youth Work (Verke) and numerous other organizations as an external expert and producer in projects, trainings, and content creation. This experience has deepened my perspective on how AI and digital culture affect young people’s worlds – and how professionals can respond to these changes.

Feedback from Talks

I have toured libraries, schools and expert events talking about where technology, artificial intelligence and people meet. The feedback has been 5/5. The themes of The Black Box have felt topical to audiences, because they are not coming. They are already here.

In Jämsä, a sixth-grade girl asked me how much water AI uses. That question was not in my presentation. I answered about half a litre for fifty questions, and I said I don’t know if that’s right. Nobody knows. She said thank you. I have been thinking ever since about what I should have told her, and it became my talk at the international Tool Fair. That is why I leave room for questions at every event. The best ones are never in the slides.
